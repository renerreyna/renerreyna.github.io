From Plato‘s Cave to Cartesian Theaters: A Perspective toward Illustrating Metacognition Through the Lens of Art History and the Underlying Neuropsychological Implications (2026)

Abstract: Art and philosophical discourse from classical antiquity such as the Iliad, Plato’s allegory of the Cave, or the formal composition of sculpture has the opportunity to reveal information about metacognition and the evolution of attitudes surrounding developmental self construction (DSC) in humans previously undocumented in academic literature. In addition to this, this approach to understanding classical antiquity as a category of art remains contemporary as revealing in nature surrounding self reference today, providing insight into how art might speak to the ongoing processes of DSC: ongoing measurement and definition of one’s place in the universe or epistemic belief, and resulting metacognitive neuropsychology (MN): information about how these people's attitudes, personality changes, thoughts, and feelings were defined by their DSC state. The insight provided from this ongoing review of direction for further investigation regarding MN outcomes discernible from the arts and DSC serves as a foundational strategy for modeling cognitive processes and behaviors from unconventional analogs of MN and DSC processes through the arts across time periods. Here I assert that Art and Text encode the epistemic norms of how people identify themselves, and that this is quantifiable with a number of measures. Keywords: Neuropsychology, Cognition, Metacognition, Classical Antiquity, Epistemology

Investigating Interpretations of Cognitive Impairment in Schizophrenia and Schizotypal Personality Disorder with the Matrics Cognitive Consensus Battery (2026)

Introduction: Predicting psychotic symptom onset in Schizophrenia (SZ) Spectrum Disorders remains elusive since it is inadequately defined and understood, the nature of its expression being hard to unravel from a pre-psychotic clinical high risk (CHR) state like Schizotypal Personality Disorder (SPD) (Goldstein et al., 2025). Neuropsychological tests like the MATRICS Consensus Cognitive Battery (MCCB) test are used to identify and predict symptoms of both, but labelling symptoms alone can be inconsistent between diagnoses. Since Psychotic and Personality Disorders have overlap in their respective cognitive appraisals they can have inconsistently interpreted features as belonging to one or the other diagnoses in clinical trials using the same test (Newson et al., 2020; Kavoussi & Siever, 1992). The question remains: how do the cognitive profiles of SZ spectrum disorders affect the interpretation of predictive psychosis results using the MCCB?
