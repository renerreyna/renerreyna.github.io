Psychosis remains elusive since it is inadequately defined and understood, hence the nature of it’s expression is hard to unravel. Natural language processing (nlp) is a machine learning based method for processing recorded speech patterns for statistical analysis has shown some use case scenario that proves as reliable if not more than standard care (like questionnaires/dsm symptomatography). Moreover, natural language processing has demonstrated reliability and efficacy in specifying the features of psychosis that identifying alone does not convey (such as bpd’s quality of speech being defined commonly as logorrhea) there is however, no centralized standard of nlp understanding or rule based attribution models to encompass the scope of what qualifies as psychotic features, and what does not from nlp outcomes. Furthermore, natural language processing outcomes are moderated in relation to the stimulus that prompts it (such as video, speech, etc) and suggest further divergence in similarity within the scope of psychotic disorder nlp outcomes analyzed. Here we examine __ studies and provide a roadmap to understanding how nlp might differ between disorders, and semantics might moderate the outcomes and interpretability.
