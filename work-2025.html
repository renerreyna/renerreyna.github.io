Corner, 2025
33 in x 63 in, Charcoal on collaged Paper

Done in one session in my old apartment. A lot of stories in this little image. That bean bag in the bottom right ended up being a victim of forthcoming debauchery. That lamp is still used, Prosecco bottles that were gifts turned into DIY candle holders, headphones someone lost and whose only artifact is their presence in this drawing. What a fun moment. That bit at the top separating the 'roof' of the work from the rest of it, and the bag at the bottom breaking the floor of it to suggest some more dimension and depth. I'm a fan of it.

Group 1, 2025 & Group 2, 2025
Both roughly 2.5 in x 3.5 in, Graphite on collaged Paper

People on the subway. Don't know why, but both of these people on this particular day decided to rock out in front of me and I had to pay homage with a cheeky capture of their aura lol. The woman on the left had some kind of snake skin jacket, fell maintained curls, and an elegantly appropriate long skirt and boots that contributed neither an easy getaway for a complete silhouette nor too drawing too much attention on their own. The Gentleman had a 'denim suit' on, which I remember thinking about "How is that so uniform, so complete, and yet not low quality or ill fitting?" I mean seriously, it was like he was right off the runway. Which is odd, because if you look this was in November. Even his shades and headphones fit perfectly. The kind of conciseness you can't really learn or teach, you just acquire after being like that enough.

Grip 1, 2025 & Grip 2, 2025
Both roughly 2.5 in x 3.5 in, Graphite Collaged Paper

Hands on the subway. Everyone has so many unique differences and features, and part of evolving my own personal style is wanting to learn how to reflect that. I guess, since we, and I included, all have our own unique way of seeing the world and being expressed (even through our genes) I wonder if it's even possible to ever truly capture someone else's essence? Or is the closest we will get our own semantic perception of what WE see from them?

Nose 1, 2025 & Nose 2, 2025
Both roughly 2.5 in x 3.5 in, Graphite on Collaged Paper

Same as above with faces. I always thought that all portrait or representational painters have a weird focus on one thing or another. It's especially noticable in really classically replicated styles like high renaissance or post modern portraiture. Between each artist, some seem to focus on the nose more, weirdly elongate the planes on a face or have a specific way of breaking down shadows on the far side of the face. Eyes too prominent, I like these features a lot because it allows you to be like hey this guy really couldt stop staring at some eyes, even when his ears were way too small.

Pumpkin, 2025
Roughly 3 in x 4 in, Graphite on Collaged Paper

Here is Jordyn, laying in my bed having a scroll. I don't have the comforter anymore, and I wonder what happened to it. Anyways, don't worry. As I'm sure you might have thought, that is indeed a twin size bed in the corner of my room! Not feng shui! I've since upgraded to a centralized full size bed. If you were wondering what my room is like, everything is white, with the exception of graphite and charcoal drawings on the wall, my brown bedding, and off white yellowish curtains. Some things like my bags and my desk ornaments have earthy colors and some natural ish levels of pigment like an old army box from my Uncle Bob, Busch x Bass Pro beer cans, Louis Vuitton sales box turned Jewelry stash with a customized outside, and the warm skin tones of Jordyn meeting her little rabbit tattoo which, although embedded within her reddish skin, maintains a slightly cool hue.

Below, 2025
23 in x 8 in, Graphite on Paper

Done in session with Peter Brown aka "Pete the Street", and was done by 125th and 12th (Shoutout Dinosaur bbq) Pete is a great painter, and I'm fortunate to have met him and shared a moment drawing with him. What a guy. We chatted about everything and he asks me "So what do you think of the art market as someone who works in a gallery" I replied "What do I think? What do you think!" We came to the conclusion neither of us had any idea of what was happening from affordable fairs to Sotheby's. I guess that's the nature of the art world, and humanity in general. Nobody can get the whole picture, but we can try our best with what we have to capture and enjoy a little bit of it with each other.
